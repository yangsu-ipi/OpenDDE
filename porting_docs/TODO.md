## Test `k2b` triangle attention on a B200 or B300 GPU

- **Added:** 2026-10-17, after acceptance line `accept/sm120-fast-25267ec` was rejected (draw `sm120-fast-1`; see [the line ledger](qualification_line_ledger.md)).
- **Why:** in the optimization kit, `k2b` is the measured first choice of `fast` triangle attention mainly on Blackwell datacenter parts: 24 of 28 bf16, head-dim-32 cells on B200 (cc 10.0) and 23 of 25 on B300 (cc 10.3), in `common/opt_core/opt_core/kernels/triattn/TRIATTN_CELLS.json` (kit commit `f4f62fa`). On A100 and H100 the kit's `fast` mostly uses prebuilt CUDA kernels, and on the RTX PRO 6000 (cc 12.0) it has no measured column and serves `k2b` only by inheritance. This port's sm_120 `k2b` row was its own choice and failed its held-out draw.
- **Needs:** a cloud VM with a B200 or B300, provisioned by the user (as for the A100 VM; see `porting_docs/step0_results_sm80.md` for that setup). Register its store in `run_store_registry.json` before its first run.
- **Plan:** development rows and development seeds (below 200) only; the held-out do-not-run list applies, including `examples/cofold/input.json` (H1).
  1. **Provision and register.** The user provisions the VM and names the GPU (B200, cc 10.0, or B300, cc 10.3). Record the GPU model, memory, driver and CUDA version, and register the VM's store (for example `/ephemeral/porting`) in `run_store_registry.json` before the first run. Install the Slurm mock as on the A100 VM.
  2. **Environment.** Build `opendde-dev` from gnode2's explicit conda list and pip freeze, as for the A100, and check that its PyTorch, Triton and cuEquivariance builds run on the card before benchmarking. cc 10.3 may need a newer CUDA build than the frozen `cu128` stack; any package that differs from gnode2's is a recorded deviation, and the results then measure that stack.
  3. **Kernel smoke test.** Stock cuEquivariance triangle attention and `k2b` at small shapes (bf16, 12 heads of 32, all-ones and padding masks): both run, `k2b` against stock and an fp32 reference, and two runs of each give the same bits.
  4. **Kernel benchmark and tile sweep.** The sm_120 scripts (`/mnt/compbio-data/yang.su/opendde-porting/kernel-bench-sm120` on gnode2: `bench_stock.py`, `bench_kit.py`, `k2b_sweep.py`, `k2b_rank.py`) at OpenDDE's shapes (the model's chunking, 128-2,047 tokens), with synchronized timing (warm-up, then the median of repeated calls) on an otherwise idle GPU, and the error against the fp32 reference beside stock's. Compare the winners with the kit's recorded B200 or B300 cells in `TRIATTN_CELLS.json`. For reference, on A100 `k2b` was slower than `triattn_exact` (kernel benchmark job 341, 2026-10-18), so the sm_80 line used the kit's native kernel instead.
  5. **End-to-end timing, if the kernel wins.** D4 and D12 with `--phase-timing`, `off`, `exact` and `fast`, alternating and repeated on one GPU, as in the sm_80 controlled timing (job 347). Report the trunk and total times and peak memory.
  6. **Persist and report.** Copy the scripts, logs and results to the NAS (for example `/mnt/compbio-data/yang.su/opendde-porting/kernel-bench-b200-<date>`) with a source manifest verified on gnode2, write a short results note, and mark the VM store retired before the user releases the VM.
- **Not covered by the test:** a `fast` claim on B200 or B300. Neither GPU is in the declared claims (sm_120 and sm_80) or has a qualification contract: a claim would need the user's declaration, its own step 0 baseline and calibration, a limit audit and freeze, and then a rehearsal and held-out draw on that GPU.
